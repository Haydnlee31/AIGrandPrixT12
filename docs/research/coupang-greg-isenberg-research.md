# Greg Isenberg: directions for the Coupang main challenge

Research date: 24 October 2026 (Singapore). This concerns the Coupang AI-first retail challenge, separately from the earlier Sites Game Hackathon.

## Coverage and evidence standard

This is a topic-filtered review, not an exhaustive archive review. The public X profile displayed approximately 64.5K posts, with full history unavailable. Six relevant X posts were read in full in the browser; the essay and supplementary LinkedIn posts were read through web retrieval. Dates use Singapore time where available. Search mirrors supplied leads, not final authority.

His predictions are hypotheses, not proof of demand or feasibility. Product concepts below are our adaptations, not ideas he necessarily proposed verbatim.

## First-party evidence

### Commerce as discovery, context, and community

[The Future of Commerce — Greg Isenberg, 4 September 2024](https://www.gregisenberg.com/blog/the-future-of-commerce)

His explicitly speculative predictions include adaptive storefronts, useful game mechanics, niche communities, creator curation, teaching before selling, visual previews, and shopping with friends. These suggest richer discovery interfaces, without establishing that any particular implementation works.

### Goals as the interface

[Five-year predictions — X, 13 April 2025](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/1911467353436463340)

Items 29, 34, and 39 propose goal-based interaction, software rebuilt around AI, and memory as a retention mechanism. Adaptation: organise shopping around an outcome and preserve context through discovery, evaluation, and basket refinement.

### Memory, action, and less form filling

[30+ AI-agent observations — X, 13 May 2026](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2054584280848769413)

He links distribution with accumulated context, highlights apps that perform tasks, and anticipates less form filling. Adaptation: remember useful constraints across shopping steps, with correction controls rather than assuming memory is always right.

### Personalisation through psychology and interaction

[2025 AI predictions — Greg's LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gisenberg_2025-ai-predictions-1-superbowl-2025-is-activity-7280227222576136192-72YX)

He predicts interaction beyond text prompting and personalised AI informed by psychology and social dynamics. Test whether a few choices, photos, or reactions reveal needs customers cannot yet describe.

### Narrow audiences and consumer mobile

[AI and the new rules of business — Greg's LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gisenberg_this-is-whats-keeping-me-up-at-night-these-activity-7299786438059970561-hOsr)

He argues for smaller niches, consumer mobile, and a minimum viable audience. Adaptation: solve one recognisable shopping situation fully before broadening. Numerical business claims were not adopted as validated facts.

### Taste, expertise, and trust

[Value in an AI-saturated world — Greg's LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/gisenberg_in-a-world-where-ai-slop-is-everywhere-and-activity-7380663076125036544-A5sD)

He believes curation, trusted brands, expertise, community, and governance gain importance. Adaptation: make recommendations traceable to product facts, relevant expertise, and customer constraints.

### Small unfinished consumer tasks

[Consumer AI and tiny unfinished loops — X, 14 August 2026](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2088272956203966970)

He proposes gathering context and preparing small avoided tasks, such as claims or switching providers, with the person confirming. Begin with one task. Adaptation: complete difficult comparison and preparation while preserving the shopper's decision.

### Agent selection and human trust

[More SF observations — X, 16 October 2026 SGT / 15 October UTC](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2110777852589191378)

Item 3 argues that an agent must select a product and the human must trust that choice. Adaptation: show fit, tradeoffs, evidence, and the approval point. Broader predictions about proactive agents remain speculative.

### Editable memory and buyer protection

[New startup-idea list — X, 24 October 2026 SGT](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/2113721373482987970)

The list includes inspecting/correcting/deleting AI memory, buyer protection, portable memory, physical product creation, and negotiation. Editable preferences and understandable purchase controls could support discovery. They are enabling features, not a complete storefront or proven business.

### Outcomes across multiple steps

[AI browser launch reflections — X, 22 October 2025 SGT / 21 October UTC](https://x.com/gregisenberg/status/1980737507747852795)

He describes people stating outcomes and agents executing intervening steps. Adaptation: connect discovery to a concrete product decision and purchase handoff. Adoption timelines remain predictions.

## Candidate directions — our interpretation

| Direction | Customer journey | AI's necessary role | What to investigate next |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adaptive visual taste discovery | Make a few visual choices, discover products, refine, buy. | Select informative comparisons and adapt preferences. | Can five interactions improve relevance and surface useful unfamiliar products? |
| Photo to shoppable room/desk | Photograph a space, preview feasible changes, buy needed items. | Interpret context and connect designs to real products. | Can it preserve owned items and avoid invented dimensions? |
| Outcome to complete basket | State a situation such as first camping trip or dinner for friends; receive a compatible, complete basket. | Resolve dependencies, missing items, budget tradeoffs, and changing constraints across products. | Does it outperform a checklist or search? Can it show why an item is needed and safely remove unnecessary purchases? |
| Product confidence and compatibility | Bring a desired item or uncertain shortlist; see the best fit, supporting evidence, exclusions, and alternatives before buying. | Reconcile product specifications, customer context, and constraints; expose uncertainty. | Can it explain one surprising incompatibility using real evidence? Does it avoid confident answers when a specification is missing? |
| Shared shopping mission | Friends choose a group activity or shared room style, contribute preferences, resolve conflicts, approve a basket. | Combine multiple preferences and budgets and explain compromises. | Is group coordination a real pain? Can the demo show one meaningful conflict resolved? |
| Expertise-led discovery | Learn a small skill, try a guided activity, discover the tools that help at the next step. | Adapt guidance and products to demonstrated needs or skill level. | Does teaching create a better purchase decision? Can recommendations remain grounded rather than merely promotional? |

Our preferred direction combines adaptive discovery with a room/desk makeover and an editable preference profile, tentatively a Taste Passport. Start with someone unable to name a desired product; learn from reactions; propose attainable outcomes; explain the items needed. Distinguish user-stated constraints from AI inferences.

The mission basket offers a clear end-to-end demo. Confidence/compatibility could be a standalone direction or supporting layer. Hobby coaching and shared baskets are lower-priority alternatives. These are qualitative judgments, not official scores.

Use one category and scenario. Ground prices, availability, dimensions, and compatibility in real or clearly labelled demo catalogue data. Generated visuals alone are incomplete. AI must change the journey based on feedback; decorative points and swiping are insufficient.

## Questions to answer before choosing

1. Who is the first customer, and what shopping uncertainty matters?
2. What can AI learn in one minute without purchase history?
3. How does that change discovery, evaluation, and the basket?
4. Can customers inspect evidence, correct preferences, control budget, and approve purchase?
5. Can the seven-minute demo prove the complete journey?
